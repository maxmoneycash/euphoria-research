# Trading product or house game?

This is an economic description, not legal advice. Classification depends on jurisdiction.

## What the money flow looks like

- The house (one vault) is the counterparty to every bet.
- The house sets the odds, off-chain, with a margin on real assets.
- On the house-generated markets, which carry most of the volume, the house also produces the
  price being bet on. Its win probabilities match a pure random walk, so economically that market
  is a random-number game, the same shape as a crash or dice game.

## What sets it apart from a traditional casino

- Stakes and payouts sit in public contracts; every bet can be audited, as here.
- Real-asset markets settle on an independent oracle whose values are public.
- It is presented as trading and paid in a stablecoin.
- The site is geo-blocked in the United States.

## Why the usual reframings do not change the shape

Gambling is usually defined as consideration, chance and prize together.

| Reframing | Why it does not change the shape |
|---|---|
| "It is trading" | A fixed payout if a price touches a band before a deadline is a binary option. The UK and EU banned selling binary options to retail customers in 2018–19; in the US they belong on a regulated derivatives exchange. |
| "It is skill" | Over 5 seconds the price is close to a random walk (fits within about 5%); on the house-generated market it *is* one. |
| "It is on-chain" | Settlement location does not change what is being sold. |
| "Players bet against each other" | Pooled betting and betting exchanges are licensed as gambling. |

## Structures that differ

1. **Play money**: free credits that never cash out.
2. **Free-entry sweepstakes**: increasingly restricted (for example California's 2025 ban).
3. **Licensed gambling operator**, with geo-blocking where unlicensed.
4. **Event contracts on a regulated derivatives venue.**
